## Problem

Plaid's `_WELL_KNOWN_ACCENTS` entry was `#0C2340` (luma=31), which is
near-identical to the LIGHT_DEFAULT ink color `#0F2338` (luma=31).
On the white bg the accent underline rendered as "just a dark line" — not a
brand colour pop — scoring D7=3, D9=3 for Plaid (weakest brand, mean=3.67).

Root cause: the old guard only checked `_is_valid_accent` (luma in [15,240]),
which accepts `#0C2340` (luma=31). It had no concept of contrast vs the ink.

---

## Fix (brand_extract.py only)

1. **New `_is_perceptible_accent(accent, bg, ink)`** — requires luminance gap
   ≥ `_MIN_ACCENT_CONTRAST` (40) vs BOTH bg AND ink. Threshold calibrated so
   `#0C2340` vs ink (gap=0) FAILs; `#1D64DC` vs ink (gap=61) PASSes; Stripe
   `#635BFF`, Linear `#5E6AD2`, Shopify `#96BF48`, TripAdvisor `#34E0A1` all
   PASS (unchanged).

2. **`_WELL_KNOWN_ACCENTS["plaid"]` updated**: `#0C2340` → `#1D64DC`
   (real Plaid blue, luma=92, gap_vs_white=163, gap_vs_ink=61 — both ≥ 40).

3. **Accent quality guard refactored** in `extract_brand()`:
   - `needs_replacement` = structurally invalid OR imperceptible vs bg/ink
   - When true: promotes world-knowledge accent (perceptibility-checked) else
     falls back to `_LIGHT_DEFAULT["accent"]` (`#2563EB`)
   - World-knowledge upgrade path also perceptibility-checks before swapping
