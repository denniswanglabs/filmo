## Formula refinement tried (the "get better" experiment)
**Tried:** vs the current best (Robinhood single-device this iter / Airbnb converging-cards from iter-4),
I pushed Shopify into a MULTI-ELEMENT converging composition AND added a phrasing nudge —
"soft blurred placeholder labels" — to the brief, hypothesizing it would tighten legibility-suppression.

**Result — did NOT improve; slightly regressed on garble.**
- The multi-element composition reintroduced a garble surface (the bottom caption strip). The
  single-device framing (Robinhood) kept text smaller + more out-of-focus and scored visibly cleaner.
- "soft blurred placeholder labels" / "checkout button mockup" likely INVITED the model to render a
  text caption rather than suppressing it — naming UI text elements (even with "blurred"/"placeholder")
  appears to cue text generation. The strongest anti-text behavior comes from NOT naming text-bearing
  UI at all, plus the canonical `_ANTI_TEXT` clause already in `cinematic_prompt`.

**Carry-forward formula notes (for the next iteration / a future code tweak — read-only this iter):**
1. PREFER single-hero-device or single-panel compositions over multi-element "converging" layouts for
   video establish plates — fewer/smaller text surfaces ⇒ less garble. (Airbnb worked despite being
   multi-element because its cards carried PHOTO thumbnails, not UI labels — photo-content cards are
   garble-safe; UI/dashboard-label cards are not.)
2. Do NOT add UI-text nouns to the brief ("labels", "captions", "checkout button", "headlines") even
   qualified with "blurred/placeholder" — they cue text rendering. Describe shapes/content, not text.
3. For brand fidelity with a SUBTLE/desaturated brand color (olive, muted green, gray), the single
   merged-hex steer is weak. Consider repeating the dominant hex or adding an explicit color-dominance
   phrase ("dominated by Shopify olive-green light") — but that's a `cinematic_prompt`/brief change,
   NOT done here (read-only). Saturated single brand colors (Robinhood green) steer reliably as-is.
